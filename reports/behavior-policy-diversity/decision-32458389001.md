# Behavior-policy diversity decision — run 32458389001

## Question

Is the rarity of H1/H3 reversals mainly an artifact of sampling only states
visited by the incumbent hand-coded policy?

## Paired experiment

For the same stream, physics seed, and scenario, compare four local-simulator
behavior policies:

- incumbent baseline;
- settled Top-K rank-temperature sampling;
- one hard-safe rank-1 divergence at step 2;
- one hard-safe rank-2 divergence at step 2.

Every alternative action was replayed in a fresh simulator and admitted only
when transport, placement, post-shake soft coverage, and priority placement did
not regress. Roots were captured at steps 9, 12, and 15, then expanded with the
same B3/H3 physical graph contract.

## Result

- 36 physical H3 graphs across 9 paired scenario/step groups.
- Mean unique board-fingerprint rate: **91.7%**.
- Mean unique model-visible-state rate: **91.7%**.
- 26 roots had at least two actions and a hard-constrained H3 oracle.
- Rank-0 was H3-oracle optimal on **13/26 (50.0%)**.
- A complete H1 root scan was H3-oracle optimal on **25/26**.
- Exactly **1/26** eligible roots was genuinely future-sensitive.
- Alternative behavior policies exposed **0** new future-sensitive roots.

The future-sensitive root is new relative to the previous 25-graph corpus:
`m-dual-preloaded-dedicated`, baseline step 9. Rank-0 and rank-2 tie on H1 fill
at `10.4835916162`, so the rank tie-break keeps rank-0. At H3, rank-0 reaches
`12.3085339168` while rank-2 reaches `13.3136559840`, a `+1.0051220671`
advantage for rank-2.

## Decision

The state-distribution part of the hypothesis is confirmed: changing behavior
policy produces substantially different physical and model-visible states.
The stronger claim is not supported by this sample: arbitrary safe diversity
did not make H1/H3 reversals more common. It mostly exposed more immediate
ranker mistakes, which a three-action H1 physical scan already corrects.

The next Expert Iteration slice should therefore not equate exploration with
uniformly more random trajectories. Search should identify an improved root
action, the behavior policy should actually follow that action, and new roots
should be sampled downstream of that search-improved trajectory. This directly
tests the AlphaZero-style claim that search changes the useful training-state
distribution, rather than only making it different.

This is local-simulator evidence. H3 fill is a bounded teacher proxy, not the
official final score, and no submitted agent behavior changed.
